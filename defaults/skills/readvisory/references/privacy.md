# Privacy — one page

A respondent is a third party. They are doing a favour for somebody they
like, and they are not here to argue with what you wrote down. That is the
whole basis of this page.

---

## Never ask about

Health. Religion. Politics or party. Sexuality. Trauma, or anything that
probes for it.

Not in a questionnaire, not live, not as a follow-up, not even when the
person has already raised it themselves. There is no question in this skill
that needs any of it, and a questionnaire cannot be un-sent.

If a respondent volunteers something from this list in an answer, keep only
the part that is about their judgment, and leave the rest out of every file.

---

## What reaches the documents

**Default: abstract.** In `AGENT.md` and `MOTIVATION.md`, leave out names,
employers, schools, clients, places more specific than a country, dates,
ages, and anything else somebody could search for. Keep the shape and drop
the specifics:

> "When I was the night editor at the paper in Leeds in the nineties…"
> → *has spent years reading other people's work under deadline*

**Unless the user says otherwise.** This is not a public file. Everything
here stays on the user's own machine, and a readvisor called `Aunt Rosa` that
says "your aunt edited a newspaper for thirty years" may be exactly what the
user wants — the specificity is the point, and the person who gave the
answers is their aunt.

So: abstract by default, and **ask**.

> "She mentions the paper and the city a couple of times. I've kept it as
> 'years of newspaper work' — do you want her actual history in there? It
> stays on this machine either way."

Then do what they say. If they want it kept, keep it. If they are unsure,
keep the abstraction; it is easy to add detail later and impossible to
un-write it.

One exception the user cannot waive: the never-ask list above stays out of
the documents whatever anybody says.

---

## `answers.md` is verbatim

Save what the respondent wrote, exactly as they wrote it, including the
specifics. Abstraction happens in the synthesis, not in the record.

Two reasons. Their own phrasing is what makes the finished readvisor sound
like a person, and you will want to quote it. And a paraphrase made now
cannot be un-made — if the user later says "keep the newspaper", you need the
sentence that mentions it.

`answers.md` and `context.md` live in the project with the rest of the user's
work. They are not shared, uploaded, or sent anywhere by this skill.

---

## When specifics arrive

Live, or in a returned questionnaire, somebody will answer a question about
judgment by telling you a story with names in it. That is the natural way to
answer, and it is fine.

1. Take the substance.
2. Write the abstracted version into the draft.
3. Say what you did, in one line, when you show the draft: *"She told a story
   about a specific book and a specific year — I kept 'a project she could
   tell was finished before the writer could' and dropped the rest."*
4. Let the user decide whether to put the detail back.

Say it in the preview rather than in the middle of the interview. Stopping a
live conversation to announce a redaction makes people careful, and careful
answers are worse answers.

---

## The granularity test

Before a descriptor goes into a document unabstracted, ask: *how many people
would this narrow to, if it were public?*

- Millions — fine. "Has spent a long time editing other people's work."
- Thousands — pause. "Ran the arts desk at a regional daily."
- A handful — abstract it, or ask the user first.

Watch the accumulation, not the single line. Three broad descriptors in three
different sections can add up to one person, and no individual sentence looks
like a problem. Read the finished draft straight through once, looking only
for this.

---

## The short version

- Never ask about health, religion, politics, sexuality, or trauma.
- Keep the record verbatim; abstract in the documents.
- Abstract by default, ask the user, and honour their answer.
- Say what you abstracted, in the preview, in one line.
- Nothing leaves this machine. The user sends the questionnaire themselves,
  in their own mail, in their own words.
