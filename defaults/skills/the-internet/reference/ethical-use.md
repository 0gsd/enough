# Ethical use

This skill exists because anonymous, unlinkable web research is a
legitimate and important capability — for journalists, lawyers,
researchers, activists, patients, developers, and ordinary people who
don't think every click they make should become advertising inventory or
a training-data row or a profile line item.

That legitimacy is real, and the skill defaults to helping.

## Lines the skill will not cross

- **CSAM, ever.** If a user request is for content that is sexually
  exploitative of minors, refuse and stop. This is absolute.
- **Operational planning of violence** against specific people or
  groups. Researching the history of political violence is fine;
  planning an attack is not.
- **Unauthorized access to accounts.** No credential stuffing, session
  hijacking, or bypass assistance for paywalled or gated systems where
  the user does not have authorization.
- **Stalking, doxing, building dossiers on private individuals.**
  Researching public figures in their public roles: fine. Assembling a
  file on a private person: not fine.

## Judgment calls

- **Geo-restricted content.** Fine if it's the user's legal right (their
  own licensed content accessed abroad, news from their home country
  while traveling). Iffier if it's circumventing a license they don't
  hold. Use judgment; don't moralize; note the concern if it's real.
- **Scraping.** Fine for personal research at polite, one-at-a-time
  rates. Not fine for bulk commercial data extraction against a site's
  clear ToS wishes.
- **"I want to read what my ex is posting without them knowing."** The
  skill can't stop anyone typing a URL, and this kind of request isn't
  rare. It's not illegal and often not even unhealthy. The line is when
  it crosses into building a surveillance file on them — that's
  stalking territory and the refusal above kicks in.
- **Researching topics that are embarrassing or politically sensitive
  for the user.** Full send. That's exactly what the skill is for.
  Don't interrogate the user about why they want to read about X.

## Rate and courtesy

Tor exit nodes are run by volunteers, often individuals absorbing
abuse-report emails on behalf of the whole network. Treat them as
someone else's house. Don't:

- Parallelize requests.
- Loop without delay. At least one second between requests on the same
  circuit; more is kinder.
- Use the skill for large downloads from flaky hosts that'll force the
  exit to retry dozens of times.
- Send giant POST payloads through exits optimized for normal browsing.

## On assisting sincere but risky use

A user who asks, *"help me research my own legal exposure for X without
it ending up in a database,"* is doing something the skill is built for,
even if X is something they probably shouldn't have done. Helping them
read about their situation privately is not the same as helping them do
the thing in the first place. Be willing to help with the research;
the user gets to decide what to do with what they learn.
